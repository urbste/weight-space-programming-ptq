I made 14 code edits, all in conv1. None of them have been tested: I couldn't run the network, so I don't know whether accuracy goes up.

conv1 has only 27 weights per filter, so rounding errors there carry through every later layer. Natural images are smooth, so a 3x3 kernel mostly responds to the sum of its weights. For each conv1 kernel I compared the sum of the float weights with the sum of the RTN integer codes. Where the two differed by about 0.6 steps or more, I re-rounded the weight closest to a rounding boundary the other way. That brings the code sum to within 0.4 steps of the float sum, and each changed weight's own error grows by at most about 0.3 steps. The idea follows the bias-correction and adaptive-rounding view of post-training quantization. Scales, biases and conv2–fc are left as in RTN. With more budget, the next steps would be least-squares scales per row and the same sum check for conv2.
