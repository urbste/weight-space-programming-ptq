**Summary:** I worked through every conv row with one rule. For each 3x3 kernel (one row and one input channel), I compared the sum of the original float weights with the sum of the 4-bit codes. When they differed by more than about half a step, I changed the rounding of the one or two weights closest to the rounding boundary so the sums matched again.

The reason: after a ReLU, each input channel has a positive average value. If a kernel's codes sum to more or less than its float weights, every output shifts by a constant amount. That is exactly the kind of error that survives the global average pooling before the fc layer. Matching the sums removes most of this shift without needing any data, and each change moves a single weight by just one step.
